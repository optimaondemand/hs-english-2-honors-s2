PURPOSE
Build the presentation that defends the game in front of a panel: one claim, the rules that carry it, evidence, limits, and a close.
WHERE THE WORK LIVES
The work is done in the Designer's Table lesson, Lesson 17: The Defense Deck. Download its PDF there and submit it here with the deck or demo notes.
STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Plan five slides. Claim, rules, evidence, limits, close. One idea each.
2
Choose three minutes of play to show. Pick the moment that shows a rule at work.
3
Get an AI critique of the outline. Keep or reject each suggestion with a reason.
4
Build the deck in PowerPoint, or prepare the demo and notes. Each slide carries a headline and one visual. Evidence replaces paragraphs.
5
Rehearse aloud twice with a timer. Plan for six to eight minutes. Watch one practice recording.
6
Log AI use. Add one complete row to the AI-Use Log.
AI-USE REMINDER
AI is optional, never required. A tool can find gaps and suggest wording, but it cannot judge whether a game is fun or fair, and it sometimes invents sources or problems. Check every AI claim against the rules, the playtests, and JStor, and log each use.
DONE WHEN · COMPLETION
☐

☐

☐

☐
