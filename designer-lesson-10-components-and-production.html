Components and Production
A game on paper has to become a game on a table. You decide what pieces it needs, plan a version anyone can print and make, and test two ideas from A Midsummer Night's Dream against your rules: hidden roles and transformation.

Choose your form and list your pieces
The floor first, extras after
●
Every tier is judged on the same rubric. It grades design, craftsmanship, research, and argument, not money. A hand-made or print-and-play game made with care can earn full marks. No printer, 3D printer, or purchase is ever required.
THE THREE TIERS OF PIECES
Floor: what every player can make, a print-and-play file plus paper, scissors, and things found at home such as coins or dice. Suggested: store-bought dice, tokens, or wooden pieces. Optional: 3D-print files (STL) for pieces, for students who already have access to a printer.
My component list
Each piece, how many, and the rule that needs it.
Pieces that hide information or change during play
Secret cards, face-down tiles, pieces that flip or swap. Say which rule uses each.

Plan the print-and-play version
A stranger can make it
●
Plan a version that a stranger could print and assemble in about half an hour. List what the floor version needs, then any optional upgrades. Use your style guide from Lesson 9 for the look.
The print-and-play file
How many pages. What is on each page. Cut, fold, and glue steps.
Materials, with cost
Everything needed to make the floor version, an estimate of the cost, and how long assembly takes.
Optional pieces
Suggested pieces to buy or 3D-print files you could add. Write "none" if none. Nothing here may be required to play.

3
The Midsummer lens
Hidden roles and transformation: use or reject
●
In the play, a mistaken love charm turns loyalties around, and a craftsman is changed in the middle of a rehearsal. Quince says of him, Bless thee, Bottom! bless thee! Thou art translated. (Shakespeare, A Midsummer Night's Dream, 3.1, public domain) Oberon describes the charm: The juice of it on sleeping eyelids laid (2.1, public domain). Hidden identities and sudden change drive the comedy. Your game may use them, or reject them with reasons.
Explain your choice, pointing to a component and a rule
Either way, point to a specific piece and a specific rule.
