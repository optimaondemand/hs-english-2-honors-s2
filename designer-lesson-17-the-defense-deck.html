The Defense Deck

1
The arc of a defense
Five parts, one idea each
●
A defense is a presentation in which you show that your game makes a real argument and that you can answer for it. Your audience is a panel of readers who have not played your game. Use a PowerPoint deck, or a demo with a short set of notes. Plan to speak for six to eight minutes. The recorded version comes in the next lesson.
Who is your panel, and what must they understand in the first minute to follow the rest?
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
Which slide best presents your evidence from playtest two?
2
Plan the deck
One line per slide
●
Fill in one line for each slide. These notes become the outline of your deck. Keep each slide to a headline, one visual, and one line of support.
Slide 1: write your claim in one sentence.
Slide 2: name the two or three rules that carry your argument, and what each makes players do.
Slide 3: which playtest moment and which verified source will you show, and what is the visual?
Slide 4: what does your game not show, and what did it teach you that you got wrong at first?
Slide 5: what does the game say about living well, alone and together?
Which three minutes of play will you show or describe, and which rule will your audience see at work?
3
AI critiques your outline
Keep or reject, with reasons
●
Give an AI tool your slide outline. Ask it where the argument is weakest and which slide is unclear. AI can critique structure. It has not seen your game played, so it cannot judge your evidence. Keep what helps, reject what does not, and say why.
Paste the AI's critique
Which suggestions did you keep, and how did you change the outline?
Which did you reject, and why?
●
Your AI-Use Log keeps one row for each time you use AI on this project. It is a requirement for your portfolio, and an incomplete log means the portfolio cannot be assessed. Write this lesson's row here, then copy it into your running log.
What I asked the AI to do
What it gave me

4
Build the deck and rehearse
Two runs with a timer
●
Build the deck in PowerPoint from your outline, or prepare your demo and notes. Then rehearse aloud at least twice with a timer. Record one practice run on any device and watch it.
What did you notice in your practice run, and what will you change?
How long was your practice run, and where will you cut or add?
