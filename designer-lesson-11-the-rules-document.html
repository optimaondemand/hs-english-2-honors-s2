The Rules Document

How a rules document works
Seven parts, one reader who cannot ask you
●
A rules document is expository writing: it explains how to do something to a reader who cannot ask questions. Four habits make it work. Define each term once and use it the same way every time. Number the steps of a turn. One action per sentence. Show an example, because a worked turn settles what a paragraph cannot.
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
Which rule sentence will a stranger follow most reliably?
2
Write Rules Document v1
All seven parts
●
Write all seven parts, using your own prototype and your style and component plans. Aim for about 700 to 1,000 words in all, then assemble it in Word with headings and your art.
Goal and overview
What the game is, how many play, and what a player is trying to do.
Components
Every piece, with the number of each.
Setup
Numbered steps, in order, ending with who goes first.
A turn
Numbered steps for one turn, one action per sentence.
Ending and winning
Exactly when the game ends, how winners are decided, and what happens in a tie.
Special cases
What to do when two rules seem to conflict, or when a pile runs out.
An example turn
One turn played out, step by step, with real values.
3
Playtest cycle 2, from the rules alone
Silence is the method
●
This cycle must run from the written rules alone. You are not allowed to rescue the tester. A recorded session or a tester's signed note also counts.
1
Give each tester the written rules and the components. Nothing else.
2
Do not explain, hint, or answer questions about the rules. Say only "read and play."
3
Watch in silence. Write down every pause, wrong move, and question asked aloud.
4
Let the game run to a conclusion, or note exactly where it stopped.
5
Afterward, ask three questions: what was confusing, what was fun, what would you change.
Questions the tester asked aloud
Each one marks a place where the rules did not answer.
Testers (number, relation)
What I was testing
Whether a stranger can play from the written rules alone.
What broke, confused, or dragged
What worked
Changes I made as a result
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
A tester stalls on setup and looks up at you. What do you do?
4
A confused new player
Clarify every ambiguity
●
Give an AI tool, such as your school Microsoft Copilot account, only your Rules Document. Ask it to act as a confused new player: play the first three turns of a two-player game, and stop each time it has to guess, saying what it guessed.
AI AWARENESS An AI can show where the written rules are ambiguous. It cannot tell you whether a game is fun or fair. Only real players can. Treat its confusion as a list of places to check, and compare it with what your testers did.
The AI's list of guesses and confusions
Confusions both the AI and my testers had
These are the strongest signals. Fix them first.
Confusions only one of them had
Say which, and decide whether each is a real problem.
