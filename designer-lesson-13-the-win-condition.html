Audit your win condition
What winning rewards, and what that says about how to live
●
In Quarter 4 the class reads about a man who built a whole life around one goal. Ask your game the same question. A win condition tells players what to want. It makes a claim about how to live, whether or not you meant it to.
What winning rewards, what it costs the winner, and whom it leaves out
Point to the specific rules that do each of the three.
If someone lived the way my game rewards, would that be living well?
Give your answer and one reason.
2
JStor find #4
The period's society, and how to live well
●
JStor is an online library of scholarly articles and books, found through your school. Go to jstor.org, choose to log in, and enter the school's shared username and password from the JStor Research Tutorial lesson. You do not need a personal account. Find one source about the society of your period and how its people understood a good life: what they honored, feared, or valued. Search the period's own ideas, such as honor, virtue, rank, wealth, or happiness, alongside your world. Log the citation, the claim you are borrowing, and one sentence on how it bears on your win condition.

The AI challenge
Rebut or revise
●
Give an AI tool, such as your school Microsoft Copilot account, your audit and your ending rules. Ask it to argue as strongly as it can that your game endorses a hollow value. Read the full argument.
AI AWARENESS An AI will argue any side you ask for, and it can sound sure about points it has not checked. Test its argument against your actual rules. It may misdescribe your game or cite things that are not in it.
The AI's argument that my game endorses a hollow value
The AI's strongest point
My rebuttal or my revision
