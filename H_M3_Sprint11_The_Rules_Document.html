The Designer’s Table · Quarter 3: the Rules Document
A rules document is expository writing for a reader who cannot ask questions. Write all seven parts, from the goal to an example turn. Then run playtest cycle 2 using only the written rules: no explaining, no hints. Whatever stops a tester shows what the page leaves out.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Write the full Rules Document v1, run playtest cycle 2 from the written rules alone with one to three testers, and fill in playtest log 2. Give an AI only the rules and ask it to play a confused new player. Compare its confusions with your testers’, and clarify every ambiguity. The work is done in the Designer’s Table lesson The Rules Document. Complete every tab there. The lesson gathers your answers into a PDF at the end.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Can a stranger play my game to a conclusion using only my written rules?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Write Rules Document v1. Cover the goal, components, setup, a turn, ending and winning, special cases, and an example turn.
2
Run playtest cycle 2. Give testers the rules and components only. Do not explain or hint. Note every pause, wrong move, and question.
3
Fill in playtest log 2. Record testers, what you tested, what broke, what worked, and what you changed.
4
Ask a confused AI player. Give an AI only the rules and ask it to play three turns, stopping where it must guess.
5
Compare and revise. Fix confusions the AI and your testers both had, and judge the rest.
6
Log your AI use with one new row in your AI-Use Log.
⚠️ AI-USE REMINDER
An AI can show where written rules are ambiguous. It cannot tell you whether a game is fun or fair. Only real players can. Compare its confusions with what your testers actually did.
