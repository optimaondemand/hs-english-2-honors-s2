The balance pass
Look for the three problems
●
Balancing means adjusting your rules so that the choices players face stay real from the first turn to the last. A balanced game is not an even game. It is a game in which the choices that carry your argument still matter.
Play your game three times, alone or with a friend, and each time chase one strategy only. Record each strategy and how it finished.
Name the strategy, the result, and how close the finish was.
Which of the three problems showed up, if any? Point to the rule that causes it.
If none showed up, say what you tried and what would have revealed one.
Write the change you will make. Then say what the change does to the claim your game makes.
A fix that weakens your argument needs a second fix.
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
In three playtests, the player who hoards resources always wins. Your game argues that hoarding is a hollow way to live. What is the strongest next move?
2
The accessibility pass
Can a stranger play it?
●
Your game must be playable by a stranger: someone who did not design it, was not in the room for the earlier playtests, and has only your materials. Read your own materials as that person would. A tabletop game needs no purchase and no printer beyond ordinary home or school printing. A digital game must open without special accounts.
Which two items on the list are weakest in your game? For each, write exactly what a stranger would trip on.
Write the change for each weak item. Be specific: the new wording, the new layout, the added example.
Name the person who read your materials cold, or say how you will find one for the playtest in the next tab.
3
Playtest cycle 3 and your log
Five fields and a comparison
●
This is your third and final playtest cycle. Use testers who have not played before if you can. Hand over the written rules and the game, say nothing, and watch. Recruit one to three people. If no one can play in person, record a session or collect a signed tester note.
A MODEL ENTRY, ON A DIFFERENT GAME
Testers: a coach and two seventh graders. Testing: does the new catch-up rule keep the last player in the game? What broke: the scoring table was on the back of the board and nobody found it. What worked: the catch-up rule kept all three players within reach until the final round. Changes: moved the scoring table to the front and added an arrow.

4
AI stress-tests your rules
Then verify against real play
●
Give an AI tool your written rules. Ask it to stress-test them: to find ties, empty decks, impossible game states, and moments when a player has no legal move. AI can find holes in rule logic. It cannot tell you whether your game is fun or fair, and it sometimes invents problems your rules already answer. Only real players show you the rest.
Paste the AI's list of edge cases
Which edge cases did a real tester reach, or could a real player reach in your game? Name each one.
Which of the AI's concerns do your rules already settle? Quote the rule that answers each one.
Write the new wording for each rule you changed because of the stress test.
