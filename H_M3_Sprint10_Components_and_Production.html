The Designer’s Table · Quarter 3: components and production
A print-and-play version is the floor everyone can meet. Suggested pieces and 3D-print files are optional extras and are never required. Judge the plan on clarity and care, not cost. Then test the Midsummer lens: hidden roles and transformation. Use one in your rules or reject both with reasons.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Choose tabletop or digital, list every component with the rule that needs it, and write a production plan that works from paper and household items. Apply the Midsummer lens in a paragraph. Judge an AI layout suggestion for clarity and cost. The work is done in the Designer’s Table lesson Components and Production. Complete every tab there. The lesson gathers your answers into a PDF at the end.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What is the smallest set of pieces that lets a stranger play my game, and what does my Midsummer lens add or reject?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Choose your form. Tabletop (print-and-play or hand-made) is the default. Digital on a no-code tool is allowed, and the design is what is graded.
2
List your components. Name each piece, how many, and the rule that needs it. Mark pieces that hide information or change during play.
3
Write the production plan. Give the page count, cut and fold steps, materials, cost, and assembly time. Mark every optional piece as optional.
4
Apply the Midsummer lens. Use hidden roles, transformation, or both, or reject them with reasons. Point to a piece and a rule.
5
Judge an AI layout. Evaluate its suggestions for clarity and cost. Say what you kept and rejected.
6
Log your AI use with one new row in your AI-Use Log.
⚠️ AI-USE REMINDER
AI use is never required. If you use it, check what it gives you against real sources or your own rules, say what you changed, and record it as a new row in your AI-Use Log.
